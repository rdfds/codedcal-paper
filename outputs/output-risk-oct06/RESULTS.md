## Root causes established by the audit

1. The original domain removed all culture objects. Restoring the qualified domain fixes assessment coverage without changing risk thresholds.
2. Applying maps in different global gauges and only translating calls afterward changes nonlinear decoder context and detection bounds. Canonicalizing before resampling and re-decoding references fixes the comparison. The same-canvas inverse controls pass exactly.
3. A shared pixel-fit selector can replace a better conventional workflow. In BARseq3 field-zero color, it selects the old barcode model with 9.21% consistency loss, while the retained stronger author workflow has 2.01% loss on the identical canvas. This is damage introduced by our selector, not a demonstrated advantage over the strongest alternative.
4. The surrogate is not an output-error likelihood. It strictly prefers a native-inconsistent transform in field-zero color. For field-one native/common inputs, resampling assigns 51.0%/60.4% of selection weight to transforms that change native outputs even though the incumbent outputs agree exactly. Weighting reduces alerts but can also miss genuine error. These measured facts do not identify whether the remaining model inadequacy is caused by mixed emitters, diffuse signal, gain or noise assumptions; those mechanisms are hypotheses, not established root causes.

The margin comparator was not recomputed for the changed incumbents; this is an incomplete comparator sensitivity and cannot establish qualification.

The culture author workflow is substantially weaker than the already tested image multistart; its failure cannot be used as a win against the nearest strong alternative. Tissue author native/common errors exceed 5% while other retained conventional image workflows recover better. Keeping a workflow fixed is appropriate for diagnosis, but choosing a weaker workflow cannot create a decisive methods benefit. This sensitivity diagnoses incumbent dependence; it is not a new favorable benchmark.
